Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man of India and People's President

About the Visionary

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam, popularly known as Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, was an Indian aerospace scientist who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Born and raised in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, he studied physics and aerospace engineering, dedicating his early career as a scientist and science administrator at the DRDO and ISRO.

He played a pivotal organizational, technical, and political role in India's Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998, which cemented his legacy as a central figure in India's military missile development programs. Because of his immense contribution to the country's ballistic missile and launch vehicle technology, he affectionately earned the moniker "The Missile Man of India".

Beyond his scientific milestones, Dr. Kalam was deeply passionate about teaching and interacting with students. Even after his tenure as President, he chose a life of education, writing, and public lectures. His humility, warmth, and relentless optimism earned him the title of "The People's President", leaving behind a timeless legacy that continues to guide millions worldwide.

Key Milestones & Achievements

  • 1931: Born on October 15 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.
  • 1954: Graduated in Physics from St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli.
  • 1960: Graduated in Aeronautical Engineering from Madras Institute of Technology (MIT).
  • 1969: Transferred to ISRO, where he was the project director for India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III).
  • 1980s-1990s: Led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Program (IGMDP), successfully developing Agni and Prithvi missiles.
  • 1998: Coordinated the successful Pokhran-II nuclear tests, acting as the Chief Scientific Adviser to the Prime Minister.
  • 2002–2007: Served as the 11th President of the Republic of India.
  • 2015: Tragically passed away while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, doing what he loved most—teaching.
